# 黄金为什么值钱？从古代货币到2026，一口气看懂黄金几千年的财富史

**Why Is Gold Valuable? A History of Gold and Wealth, from Ancient Money to 2026**

全文中英文逐段对照版 · 编辑日期：2026年9月13日 · 市场数据截至2026年第二季度

A complete Chinese–English parallel edition · Edited September 13, 2026 · Market data through the second quarter of 2026

## 引言：一块不能吃的金属，为什么能换来财富？

**Introduction: Why Can a Metal You Cannot Eat Be Exchanged for Wealth?**

假设你在荒野里捡到一块黄金。它不能吃、不能喝，也不能直接解决你的温饱问题。但几千年来，人们却一直愿意用粮食、土地、商品，甚至劳动力去换黄金。

Imagine finding a piece of gold in the wilderness. You cannot eat it or drink it, and it cannot directly provide food or shelter. Yet for thousands of years, people have been willing to exchange grain, land, goods, and even their labor for it.

到了2026年，我们已经有纸币、银行卡、手机支付、股票、债券，甚至加密货币，黄金却仍然出现在各国央行的储备中。2026年第二季度，全球黄金总需求（含场外交易）约为1,269吨，央行及其他官方机构净购金约289吨，当季LBMA下午基准金价均值约为每盎司4,506美元。[世界黄金协会：2026年第二季度报告](https://www.gold.org/goldhub/research/gold-demand-trends/gold-demand-trends-q2-2026)

By 2026, we have banknotes, bank cards, mobile payments, stocks, bonds, and even cryptocurrencies. Yet gold remains part of central bank reserves around the world. In the second quarter of 2026, total global gold demand, including over-the-counter activity, was approximately 1,269 tonnes. Central banks and other official institutions purchased roughly 289 tonnes on a net basis, while the quarterly average LBMA Gold Price PM was about US$4,506 per troy ounce. [World Gold Council: Q2 2026 Report](https://www.gold.org/goldhub/research/gold-demand-trends/gold-demand-trends-q2-2026)

这就引出了真正的问题：黄金到底凭什么值钱？要回答它，我们需要同时理解黄金的物理特性、人类的交易方式，以及人们如何建立和维持对财富的信任。

This raises the central question: what makes gold valuable? The answer involves its physical properties, the ways people exchange goods, and how societies establish and sustain trust in wealth.

## 一、黄金成为货币以前：稀缺、耐久与共同认可

**1. Before Gold Became Money: Scarcity, Durability, and Shared Acceptance**

黄金的价值并不是从金币出现的那一天才开始的。早在标准化铸币以前，人们就把黄金用于装饰、礼仪和财富储藏。它明亮的颜色、稀少的供应，以及不易腐蚀的特性，使它能够跨越很长的时间保存下来。

Gold did not first become valuable on the day someone minted a gold coin. Long before standardized coinage, people used it for ornament, ceremony, and storing wealth. Its bright appearance, limited supply, and resistance to corrosion allowed it to endure over long periods.

从交易的角度看，一种容易腐烂、难以分割，或者运输成本过高的商品，很难长期充当普遍接受的交换媒介。贵金属逐渐受到重视，是因为它们比较耐久，可以按重量分割，也能把较高的价值集中在较小的体积里。黄金尤其突出：它化学性质稳定，埋在土里很久也通常不会像铁那样生锈。

From a trading perspective, a good that spoils easily, cannot be divided conveniently, or is expensive to transport is poorly suited to widespread use as a medium of exchange. Precious metals gained importance because they were relatively durable, could be divided by weight, and concentrated considerable value in a small volume. Gold stood out for its chemical stability: even after a long time underground, it generally does not rust as iron does.

不过，货币的历史并不是所有社会都按照“以物易物—黄金—纸币”的顺序发展。记账、债务、实物支付和金属货币曾长期并存。黄金成为重要货币材料，是多种交易需求和制度安排共同作用的结果。

The history of money, however, did not follow a universal sequence from barter to gold to paper. Accounting, debt, payments in goods, and metal money coexisted for long periods. Gold became an important monetary material through the interaction of trading needs and institutional arrangements.

即使黄金本身受到认可，交易仍有一个现实问题：每次交换都要称重量、验纯度。你接受了一块金属，却未必知道其中到底有多少黄金。要让交易更方便，人们需要一种可以反复辨认和接受的标准。

Even when people valued gold, transactions presented a practical problem: someone had to weigh the metal and assess its purity. Accepting a piece of metal did not necessarily tell you how much gold it contained. Making trade easier required a standard that people could repeatedly recognize and accept.

## 二、吕底亚的创新：让金属拥有可以识别的标准

**2. Lydia’s Innovation: Giving Metal a Recognizable Standard**

大约公元前7世纪，在今天土耳其西部的吕底亚及邻近地区，出现了早期铸币。这些硬币常用琥珀金制作，也就是金和银的合金。它们的重要性在于：重量、印记和发行权威开始结合起来，让一块金属更容易成为交易双方共同认可的支付工具。

Around the seventh century BCE, early coins appeared in Lydia and neighboring areas in what is now western Turkey. They were often made of electrum, an alloy of gold and silver. Their significance lay in bringing weight, a recognizable stamp, and an issuing authority together, making a piece of metal easier for both sides of a transaction to accept as payment.

大约公元前6世纪，吕底亚出现分别以黄金和白银铸造的硬币，通常与国王克罗伊斯联系在一起。带有狮子和公牛图案的金币，是这一变化的著名例子。早期铸币的具体归属和年代仍有研究上的细节，不宜把整个过程简单归功于某一个人。[大英博物馆：货币展厅指南](https://www.britishmuseum.org/sites/default/files/2021-05/Money_Gallery_LPG_2020_Room_68.pdf)

Around the sixth century BCE, Lydia began producing separate gold and silver coins, commonly associated with King Croesus. Gold coins bearing a lion and a bull are a well-known example of this development. Questions about the attribution and dating of early coinage remain, so the entire process should not be credited too simply to one individual. [British Museum: Money Gallery Guide](https://www.britishmuseum.org/sites/default/files/2021-05/Money_Gallery_LPG_2020_Room_68.pdf)

真正重要的变化，是人们不再只面对一块需要重新检验的金属，而是面对一种带有公认标识的价值单位。铸币并没有创造信任本身，却让信任更容易被识别、传递和重复使用。

The crucial change was that people were no longer dealing only with a piece of metal that needed fresh examination. They could recognize a unit of value carrying an accepted mark. Coinage did not invent trust, but it made trust easier to identify, transmit, and use repeatedly.

随后，波斯以及希腊世界的不同政权继续发展金属铸币制度。黄金逐渐从贵重材料变成兼具支付、储藏和权力象征功能的货币资产。

Persia and various states in the Greek world subsequently developed metal coinage further. Gold increasingly served not only as a precious material but also as a monetary asset used for payment, storing wealth, and expressing political authority.

## 三、罗马与索利都斯：稳定为什么能够创造信任？

**3. Rome and the Solidus: How Stability Builds Trust**

到了罗马时代，黄金已经深度嵌入帝国的财政和财富体系。金币可以用于大额支付、军政支出和财富储藏，但日常生活中的小额交易仍然需要银币、铜币及其他支付方式。

By the Roman period, gold was deeply embedded in imperial finance and wealth. Gold coins could be used for large payments, military and administrative spending, and storing wealth, while everyday transactions still required silver coins, copper coins, and other forms of payment.

公元4世纪，君士坦丁一世推广的索利都斯金币，标准重量约为4.5克。这种金币及其后续形式在很长时期内具有重要影响。大都会艺术博物馆收藏的一枚公元313—315年的君士坦丁金币，重量为4.47克，为这一历史提供了直观的实物例证。[大都会艺术博物馆：君士坦丁一世索利都斯金币](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/920945)

In the fourth century CE, the solidus promoted by Constantine I had a standard weight of approximately 4.5 grams. This coin and its later forms remained influential for a long time. A Constantinian gold solidus dated 313–315 CE in the Metropolitan Museum of Art weighs 4.47 grams, providing a tangible example of this history. [The Metropolitan Museum of Art: Gold Solidus of Constantine I](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/920945)

当一种金币能够较长时间维持可靠的重量和成色，商人就更容易相信它会被其他人接受。发行者仍然重要，但交易者也会关心最直接的问题：这枚钱到底含有多少贵金属？它在下一个市场里还能不能花出去？

When a gold coin maintains a reliable weight and purity over time, merchants can more readily trust that others will accept it. The issuer still matters, but traders also care about immediate questions: how much precious metal does this coin contain, and will they be able to spend it in the next market?

这已经接近货币的一项核心逻辑：我愿意接受它，不仅因为我认为它有价值，也因为我相信别人会接受它。黄金的物理特性与这种共同预期结合，形成了持久的货币信任。

This approaches a central principle of money: I accept it not only because I believe it has value, but also because I expect others to accept it. Gold’s physical properties, combined with that shared expectation, helped sustain trust in it as money.

## 四、中世纪贸易：黄金如何跨越国界？

**4. Medieval Trade: How Gold Crossed Borders**

西罗马帝国消失了，但黄金没有消失。拜占庭、伊斯兰世界以及欧洲的贸易城市，继续发行和使用自己的金币。政治版图不断变化，黄金却仍然能够连接相距遥远的市场。

The Western Roman Empire disappeared, but gold did not. Byzantium, the Islamic world, and European trading cities continued to issue and use their own gold coins. Political boundaries kept changing, while gold continued to connect distant markets.

1252年出现的佛罗伦萨弗罗林金币，以及1284年开始发行的威尼斯达克特金币，成为中世纪欧洲贸易的重要货币。稳定的金属含量和熟悉的外观，帮助这些金币获得了跨地区的认可。[德国联邦银行：近代早期金币资料](https://www.bundesbank.de/resource/blob/607670/b52f7cb474e019a94f14ac3f768c99a2/mL/early-modern-gold-coins-data.pdf)；[大英博物馆：货币展厅指南](https://www.britishmuseum.org/sites/default/files/2021-05/Money_Gallery_LPG_2020_Room_68.pdf)

The Florentine florin, introduced in 1252, and the Venetian ducat, first issued in 1284, became important currencies in medieval European trade. Consistent metal content and familiar designs helped them gain acceptance across regions. [Deutsche Bundesbank: Early Modern Gold Coins](https://www.bundesbank.de/resource/blob/607670/b52f7cb474e019a94f14ac3f768c99a2/mL/early-modern-gold-coins-data.pdf); [British Museum: Money Gallery Guide](https://www.britishmuseum.org/sites/default/files/2021-05/Money_Gallery_LPG_2020_Room_68.pdf)

远方的商人未必熟悉某位统治者，但可以检验金币的重量和成色。发行城市的信誉、商业网络和贵金属本身的价值，共同支撑了接受度。黄金因此具备一种不完全依附于单一国家的信任基础。

A distant merchant might know little about a particular ruler, but could assess a coin’s weight and purity. The issuing city’s reputation, commercial networks, and the value of the metal all supported acceptance. Gold thus acquired a basis of trust that was not wholly dependent on any one state.

这种跨地区被认可的能力，是理解黄金后来成为国际储备资产的一条重要线索：它可以处在不同制度之间，而不必只依靠某一个发行者兑现承诺。

This ability to gain acceptance across regions helps explain gold’s later role as an international reserve asset. It can function across different systems without depending solely on a promise from a single issuer.

## 五、全球贸易与纸币：为什么人们开始用“代表黄金的纸”？

**5. Global Trade and Paper Money: Why Use Paper That Represents Gold?**

15世纪以后，远洋航行和全球贸易扩张，黄金与白银被卷入更广泛的国际流通。不过，历史上很少存在“只有黄金在流通”的时代。黄金、白银、铜币以及汇票等信用工具，往往承担不同层次的交易需求。

From the fifteenth century onward, oceanic voyages and expanding global trade drew gold and silver into wider international circulation. Yet there were few periods when gold alone served as money. Gold, silver, copper coins, and credit instruments such as bills of exchange often met different kinds of payment needs.

黄金适合储藏较大价值，也能用于国际结算，但实物运输始终存在成本和风险。如果每一笔大额交易都要搬运金属，商人就需要支付运输、保管、检验和安全保护的费用。

Gold was suitable for storing substantial value and settling international payments, but transporting the physical metal always involved costs and risks. Moving metal for every large transaction meant paying for transport, storage, verification, and security.

于是，一种更方便的安排逐渐发展起来：把金属保存在可信赖的机构里，让可以兑换金属的凭证在市场上流通。只要持有人相信凭证能够按约定兑换，它就可以承担一部分支付功能。

A more convenient arrangement gradually developed: keep the metal with a trusted institution and allow claims redeemable for that metal to circulate. As long as holders believed those claims could be redeemed on the agreed terms, they could perform some of the functions of money.

这并不是全球纸币起源的完整解释，却有助于理解欧洲后来形成的可兑换银行券制度。随着制度发展，黄金从人们手中直接交换的金属，逐渐变成货币体系背后的兑换基础。

This is not a complete account of the origins of paper money worldwide, but it helps explain the redeemable banknote systems that later developed in Europe. As those systems evolved, gold increasingly served as the basis for redemption behind money, rather than as metal passed directly from hand to hand.

## 六、金本位：让不同货币拥有共同的计价基础

**6. The Gold Standard: Giving Different Currencies a Common Basis**

1816年的英国铸币改革，为正式的金本位制度奠定了法律基础；1821年，黄金兑换恢复，制度得以充分运行。19世纪后期，越来越多国家采用金本位，黄金成为不同货币之间的重要共同标准。[英国皇家铸币厂：1816年铸币法与金本位](https://production.royalmintmuseum.org.uk/collect/coin-history-and-tradition/1816-coinage-act-and-the-gold-standard/)

Britain’s coinage reform of 1816 established a legal foundation for the formal gold standard. Gold convertibility resumed in 1821, bringing the system fully into operation. During the later nineteenth century, more countries adopted gold standards, making gold an important common reference for different currencies. [The Royal Mint: The 1816 Coinage Act and the Gold Standard](https://production.royalmintmuseum.org.uk/collect/coin-history-and-tradition/1816-coinage-act-and-the-gold-standard/)

金本位的基本思想是：货币单位对应一定数量的黄金，并在制度规定下维持兑换。如果两种货币分别对应确定的含金量，它们之间的汇率就有了相对明确的参照。这种安排有助于降低跨境交易中的部分汇率不确定性。

The basic idea of a gold standard is that a monetary unit corresponds to a specified amount of gold, with convertibility maintained under the system’s rules. If two currencies each represent a defined quantity of gold, there is a relatively clear reference for their exchange rate. This arrangement can reduce some of the exchange-rate uncertainty involved in cross-border trade.

但稳定的承诺也意味着约束。经济会扩张，银行信用会变化，战争和金融危机会增加对资金的需求，而黄金储备无法根据这些需要迅速增长。维持兑换、稳定银行体系与应对经济衰退之间，可能出现尖锐冲突。

Yet a promise of stability also imposes constraints. Economies expand, bank credit changes, and wars or financial crises increase the demand for funds, while gold reserves cannot grow quickly in response. Maintaining convertibility can come into sharp conflict with stabilizing banks and responding to recession.

第一次世界大战使许多国家暂停或限制黄金兑换。战后，一些国家尝试恢复金本位，但恢复过程困难重重，最终又受到大萧条的冲击。英国在1931年退出金本位，是这一制度瓦解过程中的重要节点。[英国皇家铸币厂：什么是金本位](https://production.royalmintmuseum.org.uk/invest/discover/gold-news/what-was-the-gold-standard/)

The First World War led many countries to suspend or restrict gold convertibility. Some attempted to restore the gold standard afterward, but restoration proved difficult and the Great Depression dealt the system another severe blow. Britain’s departure in 1931 was an important point in its breakdown. [The Royal Mint: What Was the Gold Standard?](https://production.royalmintmuseum.org.uk/invest/discover/gold-news/what-was-the-gold-standard/)

这段历史说明，黄金可以为货币提供约束，却不能自动解决经济管理中的所有问题。当固定兑换承诺与现实压力冲突时，政府可能选择改变制度。

This history shows that gold can impose discipline on money, but cannot automatically solve every problem of economic management. When a fixed redemption promise conflicts with practical pressures, governments may choose to change the system.

## 七、布雷顿森林体系：黄金退到美元背后

**7. Bretton Woods: Gold Moves Behind the Dollar**

1944年，布雷顿森林会议为战后的国际货币制度确定了框架。美元与黄金保持每盎司35美元的官方联系，其他货币则围绕美元维持可调整的固定汇率。黄金不再需要进入普通人的钱包，却仍然处于国际货币体系的基础位置。[美联储历史：布雷顿森林体系的建立](https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/bretton-woods-created)

In 1944, the Bretton Woods conference established the framework for the postwar international monetary system. The dollar maintained an official link to gold at US$35 per troy ounce, while other currencies maintained adjustable fixed exchange rates against the dollar. Gold no longer needed to circulate in ordinary people’s wallets, yet it remained foundational to the international monetary system. [Federal Reserve History: Creation of the Bretton Woods System](https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/bretton-woods-created)

这里有一个重要区别：美元兑黄金的官方安排主要面向外国货币当局，并不意味着普通个人可以随时拿着美元，以每盎司35美元向美国政府兑换黄金。

An important distinction is that official dollar-to-gold convertibility was principally available to foreign monetary authorities. It did not mean that an ordinary person could take dollars to the U.S. government at any time and obtain gold at US$35 per ounce.

随着全球贸易扩大，国际市场需要更多美元用于支付和储备。然而，海外持有的美元不断增加，美国黄金储备却有限。市场和各国货币当局开始怀疑：美国是否有足够黄金，兑现所有潜在的兑换要求？

As global trade expanded, the international economy needed more dollars for payments and reserves. But dollar holdings abroad kept growing while U.S. gold reserves were limited. Markets and monetary authorities increasingly questioned whether the United States could meet all potential demands for redemption.

这种矛盾最终动摇了体系的可信度。美元越是承担全球储备货币的责任，对其黄金兑换承诺的压力就越难忽视。

That tension ultimately undermined confidence in the system. The more the dollar served as a global reserve currency, the harder it became to ignore the pressure on its promise of gold convertibility.

## 八、1971年：美元与黄金脱钩，价格走向市场

**8. 1971: The Dollar Breaks Its Gold Link and Pricing Moves Toward the Market**

1971年8月15日，美国总统理查德·尼克松宣布暂停美元对黄金的官方兑换，通常被称为“关闭黄金窗口”。这一决定切断了布雷顿森林体系的核心兑换机制。到1973年，主要货币相继转向浮动汇率，原有固定汇率安排进一步瓦解。[美联储历史：美元停止兑换黄金](https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/gold-convertibility-ends)；[美联储历史：史密森协定](https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/smithsonian-agreement)

On August 15, 1971, U.S. President Richard Nixon suspended official dollar convertibility into gold, commonly described as “closing the gold window.” The decision severed the central redemption mechanism of Bretton Woods. By 1973, major currencies had moved toward floating exchange rates, further dismantling the earlier arrangement. [Federal Reserve History: Nixon Ends Dollar Convertibility to Gold](https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/gold-convertibility-ends); [Federal Reserve History: The Smithsonian Agreement](https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/smithsonian-agreement)

黄金并不是在1971年才第一次拥有市场价格；此前已经存在私人黄金市场，1968年以后还存在官方与私人市场并行的双层安排。1971年的关键意义，是美元不再维持原先面向外国货币当局的黄金兑换承诺。[美联储历史：布雷顿森林体系的运行](https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/bretton-woods-launched)

Gold did not acquire a market price for the first time in 1971. Private gold markets already existed, and a two-tier arrangement separating official transactions from the private market had operated since 1968. The significance of 1971 was the end of the dollar’s previous gold-redemption commitment to foreign monetary authorities. [Federal Reserve History: Launch of the Bretton Woods System](https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/bretton-woods-launched)

此后，黄金越来越以独立资产的方式定价。供需、通胀预期、实际利率、美元走势以及危机中的资金需求，都会影响投资者愿意支付的价格。

Thereafter, gold was increasingly priced as an independent asset. Supply and demand, inflation expectations, real interest rates, movements in the dollar, and demand for liquidity during crises all influence what investors are willing to pay.

## 九、从1970年代到21世纪：黄金并不是只涨不跌

**9. From the 1970s to the Twenty-First Century: Gold Does Not Only Rise**

原对话列出的1971年黄金年均价约为每盎司40.8美元，1980年约为614.8美元。1970年代的高通胀和经济动荡，是理解这段涨势的重要背景。但年均价只是全年价格的平均水平，并不是某一天的最高成交价。

The figures retained from the original conversation put gold’s annual average price at approximately US$40.8 per troy ounce in 1971 and US$614.8 in 1980. High inflation and economic instability during the 1970s provide important context for that rise. An annual average, however, represents the average price across a year, not the highest price reached on a particular day.

随后，黄金经历了漫长的调整。通胀受到抑制、持有其他资产的吸引力变化，以及利率和美元环境的转变，都可能影响黄金需求。黄金不会自动产生利息或企业利润，因此持有它的机会成本始终是投资者需要考虑的问题。

Gold subsequently experienced a prolonged adjustment. Lower inflation, changes in the appeal of other assets, and shifts in interest rates and the dollar can all affect demand for gold. Gold does not itself generate interest or corporate earnings, so the opportunity cost of holding it remains relevant to investors.

进入21世纪，互联网泡沫破裂、2008年全球金融危机、量化宽松、疫情与高通胀等一系列冲击，使黄金多次成为人们讨论避险和购买力保护时的焦点。但避险需求不意味着价格在每一次危机中都会立即上涨；当投资者急需现金时，黄金也可能被卖出。

In the twenty-first century, the bursting of the dot-com bubble, the 2008 global financial crisis, quantitative easing, the pandemic, and high inflation repeatedly brought gold into discussions about safety and protecting purchasing power. Demand for a haven does not mean its price rises immediately in every crisis. Investors may also sell gold when they urgently need cash.

理解黄金，既要看到长期上涨的阶段，也要看到下跌和多年横盘的时期。只选取起点很低、终点很高的两个数字，容易忽略真实持有过程中的波动。

Understanding gold requires looking at periods of decline and years of sideways movement as well as sustained advances. Choosing only a low starting point and a high endpoint can conceal the volatility experienced along the way.

One important reason central banks hold gold is that physical gold is not itself a debt issued by a company or a state. It does not depend on a debtor paying principal and interest on schedule. This gives it characteristics different from those of bonds or bank deposits.

不过，“黄金本身没有发行者的违约风险”不等于所有黄金持有方式都没有风险。价格波动、保管安排，以及通过金融产品持有黄金时的产品结构，仍然会影响实际结果。黄金的储备价值，不能简单理解为任何时候购买都不会亏损。

However, the absence of an issuer’s default risk in gold itself does not make every way of holding gold risk-free. Gold’s role as a reserve asset does not mean that buying it at any time guarantees against loss.

## 十三、回到最初的问题：黄金为什么值钱？

**13. Returning to the Original Question: Why Is Gold Valuable?**

黄金稀缺、耐久、可分割，也可以通过检验确认成色；它能在较小体积中保存较高价值。这些特点解释了它为什么适合被人们反复选择，但并不能单独解释它延续几千年的社会地位。

Gold is scarce, durable, divisible, and can be tested for purity. It stores considerable value in a small volume. These qualities help explain why people have repeatedly chosen it, but cannot alone explain its social standing over thousands of years.

更深一层，是信任的积累。从商人接受一枚熟悉的金币，到银行承诺兑换，再到央行把黄金放进储备，黄金的角色不断改变，人们对它的认可却在不同制度之间延续下来。

At a deeper level lies the accumulation of trust. From merchants accepting a familiar gold coin, to banks promising redemption, to central banks including gold in their reserves, its role has repeatedly changed while acceptance has carried across different systems.

当人们担忧购买力、金融稳定或未来的不确定性时，黄金往往会重新进入视野。它不能消除这些担忧，也不能保证持有者在每一个时间段都获得收益，但它提供了一种不直接依赖单一发行者偿债承诺的资产形式。

When people worry about purchasing power, financial stability, or an uncertain future, gold often returns to their attention. It cannot eliminate those concerns or guarantee a positive return over every period, but it offers an asset that does not directly depend on a single issuer’s promise to repay a debt.

从每盎司35美元的官方兑换价格，到后来由市场形成的四千多美元价格，表面上是数字的变化，背后却包含货币制度、美元购买力、资产需求和风险预期的长期变化。官方固定价格与市场均价也并非完全相同的比较口径。

The movement from an official redemption price of US$35 per ounce to later market prices above US$4,000 is more than a change in numbers. It reflects long-term changes in monetary systems, the dollar’s purchasing power, asset demand, and perceptions of risk. An official fixed price and a market average are also not strictly equivalent measures.

黄金的历史，照见的是人类如何在不确定中保存财富、建立信任，并把价值从今天带向未来。也许，这正是它在几千年后仍然没有退出历史舞台的重要原因。

The history of gold reveals how people seek to preserve wealth, establish trust, and carry value from the present into an uncertain future. Perhaps that is an important reason it remains part of our world after thousands of years.

本表混合保留值与补录值，尤其2000年、2010年和2020年沿用原对话数值，没有用补录来源中的不同数值覆盖。此处理优先遵守保留已知原值的要求，但意味着全表并非同一统计基准下的严格连续序列。表中R代码标明这些保留值及其来源状态。

The table combines retained figures with newly filled values. In particular, the original figures for 2000, 2010, and 2020 were not replaced by the different figures in the supplementary source. This prioritizes preserving the known original values, but means the table is not a strictly continuous series calculated on one statistical basis. The R code identifies these retained figures and their provenance.

“盎司”在本文金价语境中均指金衡盎司。一金衡盎司约为31.1035克，与普通日常计量中的常衡盎司不同。美元价格也不能直接等同于人民币每克价格；换算需要同时考虑汇率和重量单位。

Throughout the article, an “ounce” in a gold-price context means a troy ounce. One troy ounce is approximately 31.1035 grams, different from the avoirdupois ounce used in everyday measurement. A dollar price per ounce is also not the same as a renminbi price per gram; conversion requires both an exchange rate and the appropriate weight conversion.

年均价不等于年末价，也不等于最高价或最低价。名义价格未扣除通货膨胀，因此跨越几十年的数字比较不能直接代表实际购买力增长。历史价格表也未计入买卖价差、手续费、保管成本或税费。

An annual average is not a year-end price, nor is it the year’s high or low. Nominal prices are not adjusted for inflation, so comparisons spanning decades do not directly measure gains in purchasing power. The historical table also excludes bid–ask spreads, transaction charges, storage costs, and taxes.

为使历史叙述更准确，本版区分了英国1816年的制度立法与1821年的恢复兑换，也区分了1971年关闭黄金窗口与1973年固定汇率体系进一步瓦解。中文段落与英文译文同步采用这些澄清。

For historical accuracy, this edition distinguishes Britain’s legislation in 1816 from the restoration of convertibility in 1821. It also distinguishes the closing of the gold window in 1971 from the further breakdown of fixed exchange rates in 1973. These clarifications appear consistently in both the Chinese text and its English translation.
